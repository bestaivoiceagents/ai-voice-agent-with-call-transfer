AI Voice Agent with Call Transfer: How to Hand Callers to a Person Without Making Them Start Over

Imagine you tell a helper about your problem. Then the helper says, “Please wait, I will get someone else.” The new person picks up and asks, “So, how can I help you?” You have to say it all again. That feels bad.

This is the main risk when an AI voice agent hands a call to a person. The good news is that it can be done well. An AI voice agent with call transfer answers the phone, deals with the easy things, and passes the harder ones to a human, along with everything it has learned. This article explains how that works, what the research says about why it matters, and how to set it up so callers never feel dropped.

Quick Takeaways

  • In a 2026 Gartner survey of 3,566 customers, 87% said it is essential that companies using generative AI offer a way to reach a human agent.
  • In a Twilio survey of 2,000 Americans, 45% said that repeating their problem to a new person after a transfer or disconnection bothered them most.
  • A warm transfer, where the human is briefed before the caller is connected, avoids that repeat.
  • The best systems pass the caller’s name, goal, and what has already been tried to the person who takes over.

What Is an AI Voice Agent with Call Transfer?

It is a phone assistant that talks like a person, listens to what the caller needs, and can pass the call to a real human when needed. The key word is “transfer.” Without it, an AI voice agent is a closed door. With it, the agent becomes a friendly front desk that knows when to call for backup.

There are two main kinds of transfer:

  • Cold transfer. The call is simply switched to a person. The person knows nothing about the call, so the caller has to explain again.
  • Warm transfer. The human is told about the caller first, or hears a short summary right as they join. The caller stays on the line and does not have to start over.

An engineering guide from the speech technology company Deepgram describes the difference in the same way: with a cold transfer, the context does not follow the call, so the caller has to repeat themselves, while a warm transfer briefs the human while the caller stays on the line.

Why Call Transfer Matters: What Customers Say

Callers want a way to reach a person

Gartner surveyed 3,566 customers in February and March 2026. It found that 87% said it is essential for companies using generative AI to provide an option to reach a human agent. The survey also showed mixed feelings: about half said their interactions were easier when companies used generative AI.

One more detail is worth noting. Among customers who did not want to use AI, the most common thing that would change their minds was the ability to switch to a human. In other words, a clear way to reach a person does not just calm worried callers. It can make them more willing to try the AI in the first place.

An earlier Gartner survey tells a similar story. In its 2024 study of 5,728 customers, 64% said they would prefer that companies did not use AI for customer service. The top concern was that it would become harder to reach a person.

Callers hate repeating themselves

A Twilio survey of 2,000 Americans, reported by Chief Marketer in 2022, found that 45% named repeating the details of their issue to a new representative, after being disconnected or transferred, as the most bothersome part of customer support. Note that this figure combines transfers and disconnections, and the article does not give a separate number for transfers alone.

Taken together, these findings point to a simple rule: callers do not mind a computer so much. They mind being stuck with it, and they mind starting over.

Transfers are common, and they can hurt satisfaction

Deepgram’s guide cites a benchmark from the SQM Group, a call center research firm, saying that 19% of contact center callers are transferred, and that those callers rate their satisfaction 12% lower than callers who are not transferred. This number comes second-hand through a company that sells voice technology, so treat it as a helpful signal rather than a hard rule. Still, it fits the story: a transfer is a risky moment, and it is worth getting right.

How a Smooth Handoff Works, Step by Step

Here is the process in plain words. Think of it like passing a baton in a relay race. The baton is the caller’s story, and the handoff works only if the baton is not dropped.

  1. The AI notices it should transfer. It may hear a request, such as “let me talk to a person.” Or it may see that it is stuck, or that the topic is too sensitive.
  2. The AI collects the baton. It makes a short note of who the caller is, what they want, and what has been tried already.
  3. The system finds a person. It rings the right team or the right person and waits for someone to answer.
  4. The person is briefed. The human sees or hears the summary before talking to the caller.
  5. The caller is connected. The person can start with, “I see you need help with your booking. Let me take care of that,” instead of asking the same questions again.
  6. A backup plan runs if nobody answers. The caller is offered a call back, and the promise is saved so that someone keeps it.

What Context an AI Voice Agent Should Pass Along

The more useful the note, the better the handoff. Deepgram suggests passing a clear package of facts, including:

  • who the caller is, once their identity has been checked
  • what they are trying to do
  • key details such as an account or invoice number
  • what the AI has already done, and what happened
  • the reason for the transfer
  • a short summary of the call

A guide from the contact center software company Cresta adds that the person taking over should see a short summary first, with the full record one click away, instead of a wall of raw text. It also says to include signals about the caller’s intent and mood, so the human knows what kind of conversation they are walking into.

There is one more point: share only what is needed. Deepgram warns that over-sharing sensitive data is a risk, so the package should follow the privacy and consent rules that apply to you.

When Should an AI Voice Agent Transfer a Call?

A good rule is to make the AI hand off early rather than late. Common triggers, drawn from both the Deepgram and Cresta guides, include:

  • The caller asks for a person. Phrases like “talk to someone” or “transfer me” should be treated as a direct instruction.
  • The AI keeps failing. If it has misunderstood the caller twice in a row, for example, it should stop trying and pass the call along.
  • The request is outside the AI’s job. This includes tasks like a refund above the amount it is allowed to approve.
  • The moment is sensitive. Upset callers, billing disputes, cancellations, fraud worries, and emergencies need a human touch.
  • A business rule says so. Some checks, such as identity rules or legal requirements, should be written as fixed rules rather than left to the AI’s judgment.

Where Handoffs Go Wrong

Deepgram lists several common failure points. Knowing them helps you test your own setup:

  • The human asks questions that the AI already answered.
  • The notes are lost because they were not saved before the AI’s part of the call ended.
  • The caller hears silence while the call is being connected.
  • Nobody picks up on the other end, and there is no plan B.
  • The call is sent to a team that does not exist or is closed.
  • A retry causes the same call to be transferred twice.
  • The AI’s behavior changes after the underlying model is updated.

One useful test from the same guide: count how often the person who takes over asks something the AI’s note already contained. If that number goes up, the context is not getting through.

A Simple Checklist for Setting It Up

  1. Decide what the AI should handle alone. Booking, changing a visit, and answering simple questions are good starting points.
  2. Write down when it must hand off. Use the triggers above, and add rules specific to your business.
  3. Choose warm transfer as your default. Use cold transfer only when there is no other choice.
  4. Design the note. Decide exactly which facts travel with the call, and keep it short.
  5. Prepare for no answer. Offer a call back, save the promise, and make sure someone follows up.
  6. Tell callers what is happening. A simple “I am connecting you to a teammate now, and I have shared what we discussed” builds trust and fills the silence.
  7. Train your team. Cresta recommends practice drills so people know where to find the AI’s summary quickly.
  8. Review real calls. Listen to transfers every week and fix the spots where callers got stuck.

If you want to see what this looks like in a real product, some tools combine this with calendar booking, such as an AI voice assistant that routes and transfers calls while keeping customer records up to date. Whatever tool you pick, test the handoff itself before anything else.

What the Research Can and Cannot Tell Us

The Gartner surveys are large and recent, and they clearly show that people want an exit to a human. The Twilio finding shows that repeating yourself is a top frustration. What is thinner is hard proof of how much a warm transfer improves satisfaction or saves money. The SQM figure reaches us through a vendor’s blog, and the Cresta guide is published by a company that sells related software. The most reliable approach is to measure your own results: how many calls get transferred, how often people repeat themselves, and how callers rate the experience.

Conclusion

An AI voice agent is most useful when it knows its limits. Customers are not asking for the end of AI. They are asking for an easy way to reach a person and a promise that they will not have to repeat their story. Large surveys back this up.

The best setup is simple: let the AI handle the easy calls, transfer early when a call needs a human, pass along a short and useful note, and have a backup plan when nobody answers. Get the handoff right, and an AI voice agent with call transfer feels less like a wall and more like a good receptionist who always knows who to ask.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AI voice agent with call transfer?

It is a phone assistant that handles calls using a spoken, natural voice and can pass the call to a real person when needed. The transfer feature is what keeps callers from getting stuck with the AI when they need a human.

What is the difference between a warm transfer and a cold transfer?

In a cold transfer, the call is switched to a person who knows nothing about it, so the caller has to explain again. In a warm transfer, the person is briefed first, or receives a summary as they join, so the caller does not have to repeat anything.

When should an AI voice agent transfer a call to a human?

Good moments include when the caller asks for a person, when the AI has misunderstood them more than once, when the request is outside what the AI is allowed to do, and when the topic is sensitive, such as a billing dispute, a complaint, or an emergency.

What information should be passed to the human agent?

At minimum: who the caller is, what they want, key details like an account number, what the AI has already done, why the call is being transferred, and a short summary. Share only what is needed, and follow the privacy rules that apply to you.

What happens if no human is available to take the transfer?

A good system has a backup plan. It offers the caller a call back, saves that promise with the details of the call, and makes sure someone follows up, so the caller is not left waiting in silence.
